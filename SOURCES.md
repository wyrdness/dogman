## Research Notes

### Reliability Considerations

**Most Reliable**:
1. Linda Godfrey's published books and articles (professionally researched)
2. Contemporary newspaper accounts (1930s-1990s)
3. Steve Cook's original research and song documentation
4. Dogman Encounters Radio (extensive witness database, though unverified)

**Moderate Reliability**:
1. Documentary television coverage (varying production quality)
2. Local historical society records
3. Online databases and community reports

**Less Reliable**:
1. Social media posts and forums (minimal verification)
2. YouTube videos claiming evidence (high hoax potential)
3. Dramatized films and entertainment media
4. Secondhand accounts without verification

### Primary vs. Secondary Sources

**Primary**: Witness testimonies, contemporary newspaper reports, original research by Godfrey and Cook

**Secondary**: Books summarizing multiple accounts, documentary analysis, folklore collections

### Geographic Research Gaps

- More documentation exists for Michigan and Wisconsin than other states
- Indiana, Ohio, and Kentucky reports less thoroughly investigated
- Need for systematic database comparable to BFRO for Bigfoot

## Recommended Reading Order

For those new to Dogman research:

1. **Start**: Godfrey, L. (2003). *The Beast of Bray Road* — Best introduction
2. **Next**: Listen to Steve Cook's "The Legend" song for cultural context
3. **Then**: Godfrey, L. (2006). *Hunting the American Werewolf* — Broader geographic scope
4. **Advanced**: Dogman Encounters Radio podcast episodes — Direct witness accounts
5. **Context**: Werewolf folklore sources for comparative understanding
